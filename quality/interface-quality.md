# Interface quality: judge the decision path, not surface polish alone

## Review in five passes

### 1. Product clarity

- Can the intended visitor identify what this is, why it matters, and the next action?
- Does the primary CTA match the visitor’s trust level and the site’s actual purpose?
- Is proof specific enough to support the claim?

### 2. Information and visual hierarchy

- Is there one strongest element per viewport or task state?
- Do headings, body copy, evidence, and actions have visibly different roles?
- Does spacing express groups and transitions rather than arbitrary gaps?
- Does the section order make a decision easier, or merely imitate a template?

### 3. Interface behavior

- Are controls recognizable, reachable, and predictable?
- Does every interaction communicate feedback, loading, success, or error when needed?
- Do navigation, overlays, and sticky elements preserve task orientation?
- Is motion clarifying a real relation or simply demanding attention?

### 4. Resilience

- Does the page remain readable with long copy, missing images, failed media, slow network, zoom, and reduced motion?
- Are mobile and desktop purposeful versions of the same user journey?
- Does keyboard focus retain a visible and logical path?

### 5. Finish

- Are crop, radius, borders, shadows, type, icon treatment, and spacing consistent with the chosen system?
- Are unnecessary decoration and repeated explanation removed?
- Does the interface feel specific to the product rather than a collection of current AI defaults?

## Critique output format

When reviewing a page, report:

1. the product/user task understood;
2. the three to five highest-impact issues, ranked by user impact;
3. evidence for each issue (specific section/state/viewport);
4. the smallest effective correction;
5. what should remain unchanged because it already supports the system.

Do not replace critique with generic words such as “modern,” “premium,” or “more polished.” Describe the visible cause and the intended user effect.
